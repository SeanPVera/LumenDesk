# Nanoleaf Shapes parity

What a Shapes owner can do in Nanoleaf's own apps, what LumenDesk did before this work, what it does now, how that was verified, and what is still missing. A feature counts as supported only when it works through the whole stack and addresses the right panels; a control that exists but cannot do that is listed as a limitation, not as support.

**Verification levels used below**

- **Simulated**: automated tests against a fake controller. Native tests use a fake HTTP controller and a recording UDP sender (`LumenDeskTests/Nanoleaf*Tests.swift`); bridge tests run a fake controller over real HTTP on loopback (`web/bridge/test/nanoleaf-integration.test.js`). Expected bytes come from Nanoleaf's OpenAPI documentation and Hyperion's driver, never from the encoder under test.
- **Rendered**: the production UI captured in CI. Native: `RoomPlanningTests` captures `shapes-room-1440`, `shapes-room-900`, `shapes-room-620`, `shapes-editing`, `shapes-rotated` and `shapes-music` against the Demo Lounge's simulated wall. Browser: `web/app/scripts/visual-review.mjs` captures `web-shapes-1100`, `web-shapes-1440`, `web-shapes-390`, `web-shapes-draft`, `web-shapes-orientation-pending`, `web-shapes-scene` and `web-devices-shapes`, each with an axe audit.
- **Hardware**: none. No physical Shapes controller was available, so nothing in this document has been confirmed on real panels. Pairing, Bonjour discovery on a real network, what the panels display, stream latency and firmware-specific behaviour still need a hardware pass.

Protocol source: [Nanoleaf OpenAPI documentation](https://nanoleaf.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/nlapid/pages/2789310530/Nanoleaf+Light+Panels+Open+API+Documentation) (Shapes shape types, panel layout, global orientation, effects write commands, external control v2, events, identify).

## Why Shapes behaved like one big bulb

The first integration (#108) modelled a controller as one `LightDevice`. `NanoleafClient` read `/api/v1/{token}` for state and effects but dropped `panelLayout`, so there was no arrangement, no geometry and nothing to address a panel with. Commands were limited to `PUT /state` and selecting a stored effect. Themes, LumenDesk effects and Music Mode therefore had one colour to give the whole wall, Music Mode ran as whole-controller HTTP at five updates a second, scenes could only remember an effect name or white mode, and orientation was never read or written. The browser bridge had no Nanoleaf support at all.

## Where LumenDesk goes further

These are things LumenDesk now does with a Shapes wall; they are not claims about what Nanoleaf's apps lack.

- One scene, theme, schedule, effect or music show spans Shapes panels, LIFX bulbs and Govee segments together, with one undo stack.
- Music Mode places each panel where it sits on the wall as oriented, from system audio on the Mac or the microphone, under a flash limiter that no setting can bypass.
- Every state is honest about its source: requested, sent, confirmed by the controller, or unknown. Hatched panels mean LumenDesk cannot know the colour, never that the panel is dark.
- Panels are reachable without a pointer: arrow keys move across the wall as it hangs, every panel is a VoiceOver or screen-reader element, and exact hex input sits beside the picker.
- Everything stays on the local network with no account.

## Where the controls are

- **Native, room workspace**: Room → Light, select the Shapes wall alone. The studio opens under the room: arrangement and status, orientation, selection, painting, designs, scenes on the controller.
- **Native, anywhere else**: the wall's **Shapes panels** row, or **Shapes Panels…** in its context menu, opens the same studio as a sheet.
- **Browser**: Room → Light, select the wall alone for orientation, selection, painting and controller scenes; Devices for pairing and forgetting.
